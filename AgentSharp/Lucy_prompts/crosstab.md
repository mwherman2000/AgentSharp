Yes. I’d distinguish these four primarily by **what the entity is**, **whether it is embodied**, **whether it has an independent identity**, and **whether it is intended to represent a particular person or identity**.

|Dimension|**Human**|**Digital Agent**|**Digital Person**|**Persona**|
|-|-|-|-|-|
|**Fundamental nature**|Biological person|Software entity capable of acting|Digitally instantiated person/identity|Representational identity or character|
|**Embodiment**|Physical, biological body|None inherently|Digital; may have physical interfaces|None inherently|
|**Identity**|Singular, persistent human identity|Agent identity, potentially autonomous|Persistent digital identity representing an individual|Identity constructed for a purpose|
|**Agency**|Intrinsic human agency|**Operational agency** — can perceive, reason, decide and act within its authority|**Personal agency** — potentially acts as the digital counterpart of a person|Usually delegated or simulated agency|
|**Autonomy**|Self-directed|Can be autonomous within defined authority|Can act autonomously on behalf of its associated person|Normally controlled by its owner/operator|
|**Relationship to a human**|Is the human|May serve a human|**Is/represents the person's digital existence**|Represents a role, aspect, or characterization|
|**Persistence**|Biological lifetime|May be temporary or persistent|Intended to be persistent|May be temporary/contextual|
|**Memory**|Biological memory|Machine-accessible memory|Persistent personal memory/context|Curated memories/traits as required|
|**Knowledge**|Learned/experienced|Acquired, retrieved or provided|Personal + delegated digital knowledge|Selected knowledge appropriate to the persona|
|**Behavior**|Human behavior|Agent behavior|**Personal behavior expressed digitally**|Characteristic/role-based behavior|
|**Authority**|Ultimately self-authorized within society/law|Delegated by owner/system|**Delegated personal authority**|Usually limited to the role it represents|
|**Accountability**|Human/legal accountability|Operator/developer/system-dependent|Potentially attributable to the associated person, organization or legal construct|Owner/operator|

|**Examples**|Michael|TDA, autonomous AI assistant|Michael's persistent digital counterpart|“Michael-as-rancher,” an online avatar, fictional character|
|**Key question**|**Who is the person?**|**What can act?**|**Who is this digitally?**|**How is someone/something represented?**|

### The critical distinction

I would make **Digital Agent** and **Digital Person** deliberately different concepts:

> \*\*Digital Agent = an entity that can act.\*\*  
> \*\*Digital Person = an entity that can be someone.\*\*  
> \*\*Persona = an identity through which someone or something is represented.\*\*

That produces a useful conceptual progression:

**Human → Digital Person → Digital Agent**

is *not* necessarily a linear hierarchy. Rather:

* A **Human** can *have* a Digital Person.
* A **Digital Person** can *employ/contain/use* one or more Digital Agents.
* A **Digital Agent** can operate without being a Digital Person.
* A **Persona** can be attached to a Human, Digital Person, or Digital Agent.

### In Web 7.0 terms

This becomes particularly interesting for your **TDA/Pando** architecture:

|Web 7.0 concept|Role|
|-|-|
|**Human**|The sovereign biological person|
|**Digital Person**|The human's persistent sovereign digital counterpart|
|**TDA**|The principal **Digital Agent** acting for that Digital Person|
|**Persona**|A particular representation/role adopted by the Digital Person or TDA|
|**LOBEs**|Capabilities dynamically loaded by the TDA|
|**DID**|Persistent identity mechanism|
|**Pando**|Environment in which Digital Persons and their agents interact|

This suggests a particularly clean formulation:

> \*\*A Human is a person in the physical world. A Digital Person is that person's persistent identity in the digital world. A Digital Agent is an autonomous entity capable of acting on behalf of a person. A Persona is the particular identity, role, or presentation assumed by a person or agent in a given context.\*\*

The **Digital Person vs. Digital Agent** distinction may be the most important one for the architecture: *personhood/identity* and *agency/action* become separate dimensions rather than being conflated.
